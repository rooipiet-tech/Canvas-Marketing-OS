statement: A mock-to-real mode flag guarding MULTIPLE calls on one client
(here `GatekeeperClient`'s four methods behind `GATEKEEPER_API_MODE`) is not
safe to flip the moment ANY ONE of those calls gets a real route — flipping
it silently repoints every OTHER call on that same client at routes that may
not exist yet, and nothing catches this because the flag is a single
boolean, not one per method.

evidence: `GET /approval-inbox` shipped on Gatekeeper and
`GATEKEEPER_API_MODE` was flipped to `real` in the same change (correct for
`list_approval_inbox`). But `GatekeeperHttpClient` implements four methods
of the `GatekeeperClient` protocol, and the other three
(`get_kill_switch_state`, `toggle_kill_switch`, `get_last_audit_entry`) were
left calling `GET /kill-switch`, `POST /kill-switch/toggle` and
`GET /kill-switch/audit/last` — none of which existed on the real,
deployed Gatekeeper at that point. The console's kill-switch screen (per
TD-10, one of the two most operationally important governance screens)
was therefore silently 404ing against production for however long it took
someone to notice — worse than the mock-data state TD-10 originally
described, since a fixture at least renders plausibly while a 404 breaks
outright, but neither is what an operator needs during an incident. No test
in this repo runs `GATEKEEPER_API_MODE=real`/`VAULT_API_MODE=real` against
a live instance of the real service (every test injects a client double
directly), so nothing short of reading the mode flag's flip commit against
every method the client-under-that-flag implements would have caught this.

catch_next_time: Before flipping (or reviewing a flip of) a single
mock/real mode flag that gates a MULTI-METHOD client Protocol, enumerate
every method the Protocol declares and verify each one's real
implementation targets a route that has actually shipped — not just the
one route the current change happens to add. Prefer, where practical, a
smoke/integration test that runs the real HTTP client (not the mock)
against a locally-started instance of the real service for at least one
call per method, so a partial cutover fails a test instead of shipping
silently. When auditing an "X now resolved" claim for a multi-method
client, diff the claim against the FULL method list, not just the method
named in the commit that made the claim.
