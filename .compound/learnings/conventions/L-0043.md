statement: In this repo's session goals, a literal "touch only path X and Y" constraint is consistently narrower than what's actually needed to satisfy the stated success conditions (CI wiring, deploy workflow edits, a shared main.bicep append) — the correct resolution is a primary-ownership interpretation, not a strict file whitelist.

evidence: On the s3-orchestrator run, the goal said "touch only /services/orchestrator and /infra/modules/scheduling", but success conditions required CI schema-validation wiring, a deployed Container App, and main.bicep wiring — none of which fit the literal two-path constraint. Resolved via a primary-ownership interpretation grounded in `git show --stat` on sibling worktree cmos-s2's actual commit footprint under an analogous "build service X" mandate (which touched 8 locations outside services/vault/ under the same shape of constraint). The resolved AC-001 allow-list ended up including infra/main.bicep, ci.yml, deploy-infra.yml, and a new workflow file, well beyond the goal's literal two named paths — and this interpretation was later explicitly approved by the human at the spec-approval gate.

catch_next_time: When a session goal's literal touch-scope constraint appears to conflict with what its own success conditions require, don't treat it as a strict file whitelist. Resolve via a primary-ownership interpretation, and ground the resolved allow-list in `git show --stat` on the most recent comparable sibling session's actual commit footprint under an analogous mandate — surface this resolution explicitly at the spec-approval gate for human sign-off rather than deciding it silently. See [[L-0042]] for the sibling-worktree discovery technique this depends on. See also main's independently-evolved [[L-0030]] (touch-scope is default-deny but explicitly amendable via a marked append-only exception) — same underlying tension, resolved via a compatible mechanism from a different session.
