statement: AFEC (feature-exposure-control) feature flags for long-GA capabilities can wedge in `Pending` state indefinitely and may be vestigial remnants of an earlier preview-gating mechanism no longer actually enforced. Gate deploy pipelines on the underlying resource-provider registration and the real deployment outcome, not on the feature flag's registration state.

evidence: Microsoft.App/WorkloadProfiles's AFEC flag sat `Pending` for 90+ minutes across two separate registration/polling attempts on the canvas-marketing-os-foundation subscription (one bounded at 10 minutes, a second bounded at 40 minutes), including an explicit unregister/re-register cycle, despite WorkloadProfiles being a long-generally-available Azure Container Apps capability. Blocking the deploy pipeline on this flag's state (the original INFRA-002 criterion's design) would have made the deploy permanently un-shippable regardless of whether Azure would actually accept a workload-profile environment deploy.

catch_next_time: When a spec/plan gates a deploy pipeline on a cloud provider's feature-exposure flag for a capability that has been GA for a meaningful time, treat the flag's registration state as advisory (check + log + warn) rather than a hard precondition. Gate instead on (a) the underlying resource-provider registration (which genuinely can block deploys) and (b) the actual deploy/what-if outcome — if the real deployment fails with a feature-related error, that is the authoritative signal to revert the check to blocking, not the flag's own state or how long it has been Pending.
