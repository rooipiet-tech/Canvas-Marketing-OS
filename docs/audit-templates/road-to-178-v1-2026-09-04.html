Read-only az against cmos-dev, subscription Canvas Internal Admin. The August audit listed live Azure state as unverifiable; it was never actually attempted. Six findings, and one of them outranks everything else in this plan.

The daily signal loop is dead-lettering most of its graph, every day

Over three days: 493 tasks dispatched, 408 cascade dead-lettered. The cause is one event, repeated: scan_profile_not_configured — 23 occurrences each for nine scanners, 7 each for two more.

Eleven of twelve scan profiles carry no source URLs. Each fails, and the failure cascades through dedupe-signal-cards → competitive-response-strategize → morning-brief-rollup → executive-brief-rollup. The intelligence half of the daily loop has not produced a usable card since the scanners were wired.

brief_published fired on two of the last seven days. The morning brief is not reliably being produced at all.

And la-source-discovery-trigger — the loop that exists to fix exactly this — is Enabled but shows zero propose-sources or probe-sources events in seven days. The remedy is wired and not running.

The dual gate compounds, and nobody was measuring the product

Each gate looks survivable alone. But Thursday runs both per draft, and a draft ships only if it clears each. At 59.9% × 41.6% that is roughly one draft in four reaching Friday — against a blueprint starter target of one in two, for a single gate.

Every drafting function reports 100% succeeded, which is why this was invisible: a drafting run succeeds when it produces output. Whether that output was accepted is a different row, written by a different function. The number that matters was never being read.

What “working” means

Mechanically checkable, or the count drifts again. This repo already enforces seven of eight.

  • 1 · Shape validates — validate_package.py.
  • 2 · Five golden evals minimum — DEFAULT_MIN_EVAL_TASKS = 5, passing eval_harness.py.
  • 3 · Safety suite clean — safety_suite.py.
  • 4 · Rubric lints — lint_rubrics.py.
  • 5 · Registered and signed — in the signed bundle from build_registry.py --sign.
  • 6 · Reachable — a loop task_type resolves to it, and test_every_loop_task_type_has_a_handler proves it cannot silently stop being true.
  • 7 · Exercised live once — one end-to-end run against cmos-dev, with a span in App Insights and a cost row.
  • 8 · Passes its own QA gates at target — at or above the blueprint's starter bar of 50% first-pass acceptance (L987–988). No instrument exists — a repo-wide grep for accepted_output_rate, human_edit_rate, evidence_coverage and learning_adoption returns zero hits. But the data does exist, and §1b.5 now carries the baseline: 59.9% / 41.6%, ~25% through both gates. W1 builds the instrument; it no longer has to discover the number.

Point 8 is why the plan re-orders the blueprint's own phases. See §5.

The pivot — and the 4,964 lines

The inert-package problem was solved by brute force, not by mechanism. That worked for ten functions. It does not extrapolate to seventy-six.

Registry lookups in dispatch00—

Every one of those 61 task types is a hand-written handler. grep for discover_function_packages or any registry lookup inside dispatch.py returns nothing — even though services/registry/common.py L186 has provided exactly that function since S10, and eval_harness.py --all has used it all along.

The extrapolation

At roughly 119 lines of dispatch per task type, wiring the remaining 76 register rows the same way adds ~9,000 more lines to one file — a file already split once this month (dispatch_text.py, dispatch_errors.py, task_review.py) because it had become unmanageable.

s16 is not a refactor for tidiness. It is the difference between “a package plus a handler plus a Dockerfile edit” and “a package” as the unit cost of every remaining function. Nothing in Stage 2 should start before it lands.

The second constraint: review, not build

76 function rows remain. At the enforced minimum of five golden evals each, that is ~380 eval tasks a human must author or approve, plus first-pass acceptance review of everything those functions produce. The blueprint saw it coming:

“Do not stand up many agents simultaneously. Unproven agents amplify each other's errors and drown review capacity in unreviewable volume. Each phase must reach stable quality — measured by acceptance rate and guardrail record — before the next begins.”

blueprint L1244 · build-order warning

So the plan inverts one of the blueprint's own orderings. The design puts measurement at phase 4, weeks 17–20. That works when a human reviews everything by hand; it does not when the thing being built is 76 more agents. Family 8 moves to the front of Stage 2 — specifically the Agent Evaluator (101), acceptance-rate and human-edit-rate instrumentation, and the four-lens scorecard. They are the instrument that makes the rest reviewable.

Cadence rules, taken from what already works here

Two sessions in flight, maximum. The drift register is largely a record of what parallelism cost: silent env-var resets, stale docs, superseded branches, a month-old block nobody saw, and — per L-0081 — two sessions shipping the same fix twenty minutes apart because neither probed for the other.

Keep the loop discipline. A spec.json with numbered acceptance criteria and written out_of_scope, a plan review, per-lens adversarial review, learnings appended to .compound/. That process is why the deferred column is as clean as it is; do not drop it to go faster.

Merge before starting the next wave. Not “PR open” — merged, deployed, exercised live once.

Push every branch the day it is created. As of this morning, 939 lines including the blueprint itself existed on one laptop and nowhere else.
