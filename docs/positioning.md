## 3. The five pillars (with proof)

Ordered by revenue reality. Every proof line is public-safe as written.

### 3.0 The maturity curve — where the buyer is today, and why the tiers exist

The classic data-maturity curve measured how sophisticated a company's analytics were. In 2026 it has to measure something else: whether people, software or agents do the work. Microsoft's agentic-AI adoption maturity model runs five levels from experimentation to an "agent-first enterprise" across five pillars (strategy, business process, governance and security, technology and data, organisation and culture), and its guidance names the structure that closes the gaps as an Agentic Centre of Excellence. Gartner's 2026 data-and-analytics trends put agents, semantics and converged data platforms at the centre. Canvas's version, in buyer language:

| Stage | What the company looks like | In the CFO's words |
|---|---|---|
| **1 Fragmented** | Excel, siloed ERPs, manual reporting, inconsistent KPIs, point-to-point integrations | "A different number for the same question." |
| **2 Integrated** | Cloud data platform, lakehouse, governed datasets, automated reporting | "One number, reconciled, every month." |
| **3 Intelligent** | Semantic layer, trusted data products, forecasting, self-service, exceptions | "Forecasts and alerts we act on." |
| **4 AI-enabled** | Copilots on governed data, document intelligence, AI-assisted workflows, human in the loop | "Ask Copilot and get the board-pack number." |
| **5 Agentic** | Agents execute workflows across systems with people approving at defined control points; governance, observability, measured outcomes | "Software does the work; our people approve." |

Value rises along the same curve: reporting → decisions → automation → autonomous execution.

**The second dimension.** Technology alone no longer equals maturity — a group can own Fabric, Power BI and Copilot and still be at stage 1. Assess six capabilities at once: data foundation (can we trust and access the data?), semantic intelligence (do we agree what it means? — the capability agents cannot work without), decision intelligence (does data improve decisions?), AI-augmented business (can AI take part in the work?), agentic operations (can software perform the work, with humans at control points?), and the operating model (does the organisation continuously decide whether people, software or agents should do each process?). The "one number for the same question" pain is a stage-1 symptom of a semantic-intelligence gap, which is why a semantic layer is the hinge of the whole curve and of Pillar 5.

**How the tiers map to the curve — the sales graphic.** Integrate takes a group from Fragmented to Integrated (sources connected, Fabric foundation, semantic layer, governance, reporting). Build takes it from Integrated through Intelligent to AI-enabled (automation, applications, analytics, AI solutions, first agents, decision workflows). Scale takes it from AI-enabled to Agentic (enterprise agent architecture, multi-agent workflows, AI governance and observability, reusable IP, continuous process redesign). The graphic — maturity stages along the bottom, value rising, the three tiers as overlapping bars beneath — explains both where the customer is today and why they progress; it belongs in the deck and on the CoEaaS page. Any maturity scores plotted on it are illustrative and must be labelled as such.

**Proof discipline on the curve.** Canvas has production proof through stage 3 and into stage 4 (Copilot grounded on governed semantic models). Stage 5 is roadmap until an agent is executing a workflow in a client's production environment — describe it as where the curve goes, not as delivered work (§7).


### Pillar 1 — The economics: an embedded team on subscription, not a hire
The business. ~80% of revenue. Leads every surface.
- **The alternative we compete with is the job requisition.** A CFO whose reporting has become a problem has four real options: hire one senior BI person (two quarters to a reconciled pack; one skill set; total key-person risk; fixed cost whether the backlog is full or empty), a generic Power BI shop (dashboards to a scope, logic leaves with the project), the ERP vendor's reporting module (one entity, never the group), or CoEaaS.
- **What CoEaaS is:** five skill sets from week one — a Chartered Accountant who designs and reviews every finance report, a Microsoft-certified Fabric engineer, a Power BI developer, a data scientist for forecasting and exceptions, and an engagement lead who owns the backlog. Bench-backed: leave, illness and resignations are our problem. Fortnightly sprints with a demo, monthly steering with the CFO or FD, Severity-1 treatment on any reconciliation variance. Everything runs in the client's Microsoft tenant; architecture-as-code and run-books live in their Git. Built to be owned by them.
- **Commercial model (Pieter, 2 Sep 2026): three levels of responsibility, not hours.** **Integrate** — *we make the environment work*: sources connected, Fabric foundation live, finance pack reconciled and CA-reviewed, core dashboards, security, one or two automations, AI-readiness assessment; one delivery stream; shared engagement lead; monthly prioritisation. **Build** (the hero tier) — *we build the solutions*: Integrate plus production engineering against the client's backlog — data, applications, automation, copilots and agents, forecasting — deployed, adopted and measured monthly; up to three concurrent streams; named engagement lead; quarterly roadmap. **Scale** — *we run the capability*: Build plus a dedicated lead and reserved pod, solution architect, enterprise semantic layer, data and AI governance, production support under SLA, executive steering and portfolio ROI; five or more streams. The client journey is Integrate → Build → Scale. The comparison changes with the tier: at Integrate the alternative is a BI hire; at Build it is a small internal team or a consultancy that builds and leaves; at Scale it is a Head of Data, an AI team and a consultancy together — which is the frame in which the Scale retainer is the cheapest option on the table. Scale is mostly sold up from Build, not cold; its buyer is the CEO, exec committee or CIO, and its trigger is a board AI mandate, a failed AI pilot, or a Build engagement hitting the ceiling of "solutions" and needing an operating model. Sold on scope, velocity, responsibility and outcomes; internal capacity assumptions never appear on a client-facing surface; usage (Azure/Fabric, AI API, licences, messaging) billed at cost. Prices appear only in proposals and in-room decks. Standing reference: `claude_coeaas-commercial-model-2026-09.md`.
- **How clients get here:** almost every CoEaaS engagement starts as a platform build — Canvas for Dynamics 365, Canvas for Sage, or a Fabric foundation for a multi-ERP group — and converts when the finance pack reconciles. Nobody signs a retainer on a promise. Marketing's job is to start the platform conversation; retention is a marketing responsibility.

**How the CoE runs (the operating loop, in plain words):**
1. **Discover** — sit with the finance team and the operators; find the friction, the leakage, the number nobody trusts.
2. **Prioritise** — one backlog, ranked by business value, feasibility and how quickly people will actually use it. The client owns the backlog.
3. **Build** — the smallest production-grade thing that changes the number. Senior judgement on every design; the repetitive engineering underneath — code, tests, documentation — is accelerated with Microsoft and Anthropic AI tooling, so a small senior team ships more without giving up review or accountability. *(Confirmed by Pieter, 2 Sep 2026: Microsoft and/or Anthropic tooling is used in development. Claim at vendor level; no productivity multiplier is quoted until measured.)*
4. **Deploy** — into the client's tenant, security model and workflow, alongside internal IT and inside its governance. Not a demo environment.
5. **Measure** — adoption, reconciliation, cycle time, the operating KPI it was built to move. Reported to steering monthly.
6. **Productise** — repeated patterns are folded back into the platform family (Pillar 4), so the next entity, ERP or client is faster.

**Entry offer:** the **Foundation Sprint** — one problem, three weeks: discovery and KPI catalogue, the six-capability maturity assessment (where the group sits on the curve, scored with the client — the full six-capability scoring is part of the paid sprint; a free 20-minute exec-level version, run by JP or Reinhardt and producing a one-page stage map and next-step tier, is the lead magnet for Scale-triggered inbound; Pieter, 2 Sep 2026), Fabric workspace live, first sources landed, reconciliation policy agreed, and a costed backlog. Then finance pack live at roughly week 13, group-wide at roughly 26, then CoEaaS. No "production in 30 days" promise on any surface.

### Pillar 5 — The semantic layer for AI: AI-ready as a by-product
The forward-looking story, told honestly.
- "Let your data find you": exception management, alerts, live KPI monitoring, subscriptions — before anyone opens a report.
- Copilot grounded on governed semantic models is in production today: plain-English questions, answered with the board-pack number, bounded by the user's security scope. Fabric data agents and Joule hand-offs run on the same layer as use cases mature — described as "on the same layer", not as delivered client work.
- The argument: AI is only as good as the definitions underneath it. Revenue, margin, OEE, on-time delivery defined once, reused by every dashboard, spreadsheet, Copilot and agent — or three assistants give three answers and the spreadsheet problem returns. Clients get AI-ready as a by-product of finance-grade reporting, not as a separate AI programme.
- AI inside delivery, in one sentence: we build with Microsoft and Anthropic AI tooling ourselves, and every finance figure it touches is still reviewed by a Chartered Accountant and reconciled to source. The human control layer is the feature.

**The agentic operating system, in plain words (define once, then use the definition; never as a headline):** an agentic business runs on five things that must exist before any agent can be trusted — data it can reach (one governed lakehouse), numbers it can't get wrong (one semantic layer, reconciled to source), doors it's allowed through (APIs and permissions bounded by the same security your people have), a place where a person says yes (control points, audit trail, evaluation), and a rhythm for deciding what moves next (backlog, monthly value review, steering).
